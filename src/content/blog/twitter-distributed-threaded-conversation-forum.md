The other day I was searching for some advice on Ruby on Rails. I got the usual links to message forums, experts exchange, HOWTO articles, documentation and API pages. One of the search results I found was a link to a [twitter account](https://web.archive.org/web/20140412062549/https://twitter.com/casperlarsen). It seemed out of place among the other resources, but I soon discovered that this user is essentially using Twitter (via hash tags) as a gigantic, distributed message forum. And he's not alone.

Just throw a bunch of hash tags ([#rails](https://web.archive.org/web/20090802174034/https://twitter.com/search?q=%23rails "#rails") [#ror](https://web.archive.org/web/20090801135539/http://twitter.com/search?q=%23RoR "#ror") [#ruby](https://web.archive.org/web/20110313103629/http://twitter.com/search?q=%23Ruby "#ruby") [#gem](https://web.archive.org/web/20130614030453/https://twitter.com/search?q=%23gem "#gem") [#railsgem](https://twitter.com/search?q=%23railsgem "#railsgem")) on your tweet and it immediately shows up in the "message forum" ([hash tag feed](https://web.archive.org/web/20090801135539/http://twitter.com/search?q=%23RoR)) for the given topics. A "post" (tweet) can be cross-posted to multiple "forums" (hash tags) at the same time. Replies come directly to you via your @username. The main disadvantage is the 140-character limit and the inability to include formatting or other media.

I've never gotten into Twitter much, but this realization presents a novel use of what I had come to think of as simply a marketing platform. Maybe it's got a broader use after all.
